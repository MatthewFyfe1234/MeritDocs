# **Strategic Policy Alignment and Regulatory Trajectories in Irish Offsite Construction Manufacturing to 2030**

## **Industrial Strategy and Economic Performance**

Ireland’s industrial strategy is undergoing a fundamental realignment designed to address the productivity divergence between its foreign direct investment (FDI) base and its domestic enterprise sector1. The Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment (DETE), through its *White Paper on Enterprise 2022-2030*, outlines an economic vision where competitive advantage is built on sustainability, digital innovation, and operational efficiency1. Historically, the Irish manufacturing sector has operated as a dual-speed economy: a highly productive, multinational-dominated export tier (concentrated in pharmaceuticals, medical devices, and ICT) alongside a larger, low-yielding indigenous tier serving local or regional markets1. The state's policy intent is to elevate domestic enterprises by promoting advanced manufacturing techniques, such as offsite construction and prefabricated building systems, to improve productivity and expand export potential1.  
Enterprise Ireland’s strategy for 2025-2029, *Delivering for Ireland, Accelerating Sustainable Irish Business*, translates these broad goals into quantitative metrics6. This strategy focuses state support on firms that commit to decarbonisation, digital adoption, and operational efficiency2. Over this five-year window, the agency aims to support 1,000 new start-ups, assist 1,700 additional Irish-owned companies in entering export markets, and achieve a total export volume of €50 billion6. Indigenous manufacturers, particularly offsite construction and Modern Methods of Construction (MMC) suppliers, are expected to play a key role in achieving these targets by industrialising traditional site-based processes and expanding their share of the domestic market5.

## **Housing, Offsite Construction, and Modern Methods of Construction**

The Irish government’s housing strategy is built on the systematic industrialisation of residential construction5. Under *Housing for All*, and its updated action plan, *Delivering Homes, Building Communities 2025-2030*, Modern Methods of Construction (MMC) are treated as a necessity to overcome stagnant on-site productivity and labor shortages5. The state’s housing strategy sets a target of delivering 303,000 new homes between 2025 and 2030, with a target pathway to achieve 60,000 completed units annually by 20308.  
To achieve this volume, the state is shifting its procurement and regulatory framework to support offsite construction manufacturing5. Under the current action plan, the government has mandated that MMC must be integrated into at least 25% of all new social and affordable housing projects7.  
The translation of this policy intent into structural mechanisms is driven by several key initiatives:

* **The MMC Action Plan**: Launched on 9 June 2025, this cross-departmental strategy focuses on senior management training, standardisation of construction details, digital integration, and certified training pathways10.  
* **The National MMC Demonstration Park**: Located at the Mount Lucas campus in County Offaly, this joint capital investment serves as a physical center for training and demonstrating structural, thermal, and spatial offsite manufacturing technologies10.  
* **Public Procurement Roadmap (Action 13.2)**: This initiative introduces standardized designs, aggregated project bidding, and evaluation criteria that reward carbon performance and circular economy principles in public residential contracts12.

To qualify for public residential contracts under the *Delivering Homes, Building Communities* framework, offsite manufacturers must obtain Agrément Certification from the National Standards Authority of Ireland (NSAI)9. This certification confirms that 3D-volumetric modules, light gauge steel frames, mass engineered timber, and panellised 2D systems comply with Irish Building Regulations and have a verified service life of at least 60 years9. This certification requirement acts as a gatekeeper, ensuring that the speed of offsite manufacturing does not compromise structural durability or fire safety9.

## **Workforce Development and Skills**

The transition from conventional on-site building to factory-controlled offsite manufacturing requires a restructuring of the industrial workforce5. Traditional construction skills are being augmented by a hybrid mix of digital literacy, advanced manufacturing precision, and factory production control9. SOLAS, the further education and training authority, is leading this workforce transition under its *FET Strategy 2026-2030*, targeting upskilling, apprenticeships, and green skills11.  
Through the *Skills to Advance* initiative, the state provides highly subsidized, accredited upskilling and reskilling programs directly to manufacturers and their employees13. This includes targeted micro-qualifications—short, accredited modules of approximately 50 hours of combined learning—designed to fit around shift patterns in manufacturing plants13. Additionally, the *Careers in Construction Action Plan* and modernized craft apprenticeships aim to attract younger, more diverse talent by promoting clean, safe, and technologically advanced factory environments over traditional construction sites11.

Employers are expected to move away from ad-hoc recruitment toward structured workforce planning, integrating these state-funded training frameworks into their factory production controls9.

## **Fair Pay, Wages, and Employment Standards**

The industrialisation of construction shifts labor from project-based, on-site employment to permanent, factory-based manufacturing contracts5. This shift exposes manufacturers to an evolving landscape of domestic and European employment regulations designed to promote worker security, pay transparency, and work-life balance17.

Under the *Sick Leave Act 2022*, employees are entitled to 5 days of statutory paid sick leave per calendar year, paid at 70% of their normal daily wage up to a maximum cap of €110 per day18. While the original legislation planned a gradual increase to 10 days by 2026, the government paused this expansion in April 2025 due to cumulative cost pressures on employers, maintaining the 5-day limit for 202617. Employers must keep detailed records of all statutory sick leave for four years or face fines of up to €2,50018. WRC disputes are increasingly centering on whether pre-existing company sick-pay schemes are demonstrably "more favorable" than the statutory baseline17.  
Effective 1 January 2026, the national minimum wage increased by €0.65 to €14.15 per hour17. This adjustment requires updates to payroll configurations and contributes to rising baseline labor costs, particularly in labor-intensive assembly operations17.  
The National Automatic Enrolment Retirement Savings Authority (NAERSA) launched the state's auto-enrolment scheme, "My Future Fund," on 1 January 202617. Eligible employees aged 23 to 60 who earn more than €20,000 annually and are not already enrolled in a qualifying workplace pension scheme are automatically enrolled17. The initial contribution rate is set at 1.5% of the employee's salary for both employer and employee, and is scheduled to scale to 6% over a ten-year period19.

## **Health, Safety, and Worker Wellbeing**

The health and safety profile of an offsite manufacturing facility combines elements of traditional construction risk with the machinery and assembly hazards of a stationary production plant5. The Health and Safety Authority (HSA) regulates this sector under the *Safety, Health and Welfare at Work Act 2005*23. The HSA’s inspection strategy involves completing 10,000 proactive workplace inspections annually, with a focus on high-risk sectors like manufacturing and construction25. Inspections target traditional safety risks, such as machine guarding, manual handling, and fall prevention during factory-to-site assembly, alongside modern operational risks26.  
Under Section 19 of the 2005 Act, employers have a legal obligation to identify and assess psychosocial risks in the same manner as physical hazards25. The HSA expects written Safety Statements to include documented assessments and control measures for work intensity, workload, role clarity, and workplace bullying25. To support compliance, the HSA and the State Claims Agency promote the *Work PositiveCI* platform, a free, confidential psychosocial risk management tool designed to evaluate workplace stress and generate structured action plans25.

| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Psychosocial Risk Assessment** | Section 19, SHWW Act 200525 | Mandatory written stress, bullying, and culture risk audits25. |

| **Right to Disconnect Compliance** | HSA Approved Code of Practice28 | Policies protecting worker rest time and digital boundaries28. |
| **Toxic Substance Control** | S.I. No. 122/202431 | Stricter exposure monitoring for chemical agents and mutagens31. |

Manufacturers must also verify that on-site assembly teams are trained in accordance with the revised Safe Pass standards, which have been updated to reflect the hazards associated with installing heavy modular components15.

## **Privacy and Employee Data**

The digitization of manufacturing processes—such as using Computer-Aided Design (CAD) files to direct robotic cutting tools, using IoT tracking systems, and implementing automated performance management—has increased the volume of employee data collected on the factory floor1. This data collection is regulated under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the domestic *Data Protection Acts*33.  
The Data Protection Commission (DPC) prioritizes the protection of employee data, emphasizing that workers do not lose their privacy rights upon entering the workplace34. Systematic monitoring of employees—including GPS tracking on transport vehicles, biometric access systems, keystroke logging, and continuous CCTV surveillance—is considered highly intrusive34. To implement these technologies, employers must:

* Complete and document a detailed Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)34.  
* Establish a clear legal basis, as employee consent is rarely considered valid due to the imbalance of power in employment relationships34.  
* Demonstrate compliance with the principles of data minimization and proportionality, showing that less invasive monitoring methods are unavailable34.

A key regional contrast exists in Northern Ireland, where the *Fair Employment (Northern Ireland) Act 1989* and the *Fair Employment and Treatment Order (FETO) 1998* legally mandate the systematic monitoring of employees' religious affiliations and political beliefs to prevent discrimination35. In contrast, such monitoring is highly restricted in the Republic of Ireland under GDPR, unless specific exemptions apply34.

The delay of the EU AI Act's high-risk provisions until December 2027 provides a transitional window19. However, manufacturers planning to integrate automated scheduling, algorithmic performance assessments, or AI-driven safety monitoring should begin aligning their data architectures with these requirements to avoid future compliance issues19.

## **Decarbonisation and Emissions**

Ireland's environmental policy is anchored by the *Climate Action and Low Carbon Development (Amendment) Act 2021*, which establishes legally binding carbon budgets to achieve a 51% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions by 2030 (relative to 2010 levels) and net-zero emissions by 20501.

\[National 2030 Target: \-51% GHG\] ────────────────────────► Industrial Sector Mandate: \-35% CO2  
                                                         ► Focus on Thermal Electrification & Process Efficiency \[cite: 1\]  
                                                         ► Decarbonisation of Logistics & Raw Material Sourcing

To meet these national targets, the state’s sectoral emissions ceilings require a 35% reduction in emissions from the manufacturing sector by 20301. Heavy industrial operations—such as structural steel fabrication and cement manufacturing—are subject to the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS)37. The ETS is phasing out free carbon allowances through to 2034, which will directly increase the financial cost of high-carbon materials37.  
For offsite construction manufacturers, who often operate as medium-sized energy users rather than primary polluters, decarbonisation requires action in three main areas:

1. **Electrification of Thermal Processes**: Transitioning factory heating systems and curing ovens from fossil fuels to renewable electricity or heat pumps1.  
2. **Logistical Efficiency**: Minimizing transport emissions during the delivery of heavy modular components from factory to site5.  
3. **Upstream Material Sourcing**: Sourcing raw materials with lower embodied carbon, as public and private clients are increasingly tracking value chain emissions8.

## **Embodied Carbon and Materials**

The regulatory focus of the construction sector is shifting from operational energy efficiency to embodied carbon—the greenhouse gas emissions generated during raw material extraction, manufacturing, transport, and assembly8. This change is driven by the EU *Energy Performance of Buildings Directive* (EPBD) and implemented locally through the *Circular Economy Act 2022* and the *Circular Economy Strategy 2026-2028*8.

### **Whole-Life Carbon Assessments**

Under the *Circular Economy Strategy 2026-2028*, the state is introducing phased, mandatory Whole Life-Cycle Greenhouse Gas Assessments:

* **June 2026**: Mandatory for large publicly funded projects, specifically non-residential buildings with budgets over €5 million and residential buildings over €30 million42.  
* **January 2028**: Mandatory for all new buildings larger than 1,000 square meters42.  
* **January 2030**: Mandatory for all other new buildings42.

### **Embodied Carbon in Building Energy Ratings**

Beginning in January 2028, embodied carbon declarations must be integrated into Building Energy Rating (BER) certificates for large buildings, with this requirement expanding to all new buildings by January 203042.

### **Recycled Content Mandates**

By 2028, public infrastructure projects and non-residential public buildings must ensure that at least 10% by weight of all construction materials used are recycled materials42.

The updated EU *Construction Products Regulation* (EU) 2024/3110 became applicable on 8 January 202641. The CPR reform is a gradual transition, with core parts of the 2011 regulation continuing to apply until 204041. A key change is that Environmental Product Declarations (EPDs), compiled in accordance with standard EN 15804, must be integrated directly into the mandatory Declaration of Performance and Conformity (DoPC)41. While the European Commission has prioritized high-volume, high-carbon product groups like structural timber, cement, and steel for early updates, "building kits and prefabricated elements" are scheduled for later regulatory waves spanning 2027-202932. This timeline provides offsite manufacturers with a strategic window to standardize their life-cycle assessments and digital data structures before compliance becomes mandatory32.

## **Waste, Product Stewardship, and Circular Economy**

Ireland’s waste and resource policies are designed to transition the economy away from a linear model to a regenerative circular system45. The *Circular Economy and Miscellaneous Provisions Act 2022* provides the domestic legal framework for this transition, establishing new waste levies, commercial segregation rules, and funding mechanisms45.  
Under the Act, the state has redesignated the Environment Fund as the *Circular Economy Fund*, which ring-fences revenues from waste levies to support industrial symbiosis and circular design projects45. To drive efficiency, the Act mandates strict waste segregation and incentivised charging for commercial waste, requiring separate collection bins for recyclables and organic waste to minimize landfill volumes45.

In parallel, the EU *Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation* (PPWR) entered into force on 11 February 2025, with first compliance obligations applying from 12 August 202650. This regulation replaces older national directives with direct, harmonised requirements50. Manufacturers must prepare comprehensive Annex VII technical documentation and issue a Declaration of Conformity (DoC) for all packaging formats used to transport products50. Packaging must be minimized, free from hazardous substances like PFAS, and carry standardized labels, such as QR codes, to verify compliance50.

## **Export Competitiveness and Trade**

As an open, export-led economy, Ireland relies on compliance with EU standards to access the Single Market1. However, global trade policy and regional geopolitical shifts have introduced compliance requirements and supply chain challenges that directly affect Irish manufacturers1.  
The EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) entered its definitive phase on 1 January 2026, introducing a carbon tariff on carbon-intensive imports from non-EU countries37. CBAM is designed to prevent carbon leakage by ensuring that imported goods face equivalent carbon pricing to those produced within the EU under the Emissions Trading System37.

The EPA is authorized to audit declarations and inspect premises, with penalty frameworks for unregistered imports or failed declarations53. Importers must work with non-EU suppliers to obtain actual, verified carbon intensity data at the installation level52. Sourcing steel or aluminum from non-EU markets—including the United Kingdom—requires compliance with these tracking and financial obligations37. Furthermore, a proposed scope expansion scheduled for 1 January 2028 is expected to bring downstream manufactured products, such as metal furniture and automotive parts, into the CBAM framework54.  
The UK-EU *Trade and Cooperation Agreement* (TCA) and post-Brexit rules also continue to affect trade, with approximately 40% of Ireland's construction product trade involving the United Kingdom55. To address regulatory differences and support cross-border trade, the state-funded *All-Island Circular Economy Forum*, led by InterTrade Ireland, is working to coordinate waste regulations, support green procurement, and establish digital industrial symbiosis platforms to match materials across the border42.

## **Sustainability Reporting**

The EU *Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive* (CSRD) is designed to put environmental, social, and governance (ESG) reporting on an equal footing with financial reporting56. Transposed into Irish law via S.I. No. 336/2024 (and amended by S.I. No. 498/2024), the directive’s domestic rollout was updated in July 2025 by S.I. No. 309/2025, which implemented the EU's "Stop the Clock" provisions to simplify compliance timelines33.  
The 2025 regulations addressed several implementation issues, narrowing the definition of "net turnover" to exclude holding activities and clarifying that ineligible entities under Schedule 5 of the Companies Act 2014 must only report if they exceed the standard size thresholds59. The regulations also expanded subsidiary exemptions, allowing Irish subsidiaries to be exempted if they are included in the consolidated report of an EU parent company59.

Even if an offsite construction manufacturer is an unlisted SME and not directly in scope of CSRD, they are affected by the reporting requirements of their clients33. Large developers, public bodies, and tier-one contractors within the Wave 1 and Wave 2 categories must report on their Scope 3 value chain emissions33. To satisfy their own compliance audits, these large entities require verified greenhouse gas data, waste records, and labor standards from their suppliers33. Consequently, manufacturers who cannot provide verified ESG data risk being excluded from the supply chains of major developers and public contracts32.

## **Government Procurement**

With public procurement representing approximately 14% of Ireland’s gross domestic product (GDP), the state uses its buying power to drive environmental and social outcomes36. The Office of Government Procurement (OGP) regulates public tenders under the *National Public Procurement Policy Framework* and the *Buying Greener: Green Public Procurement Strategy and Action Plan 2024-2027*40.  
Published in July 2025, Circular 17/2025 replaces voluntary guidelines with a "comply or explain" mandate for Green Public Procurement (GPP)61:

* **Scope**: Applies to all public competitions for goods and services valued over €50,000, and works contracts valued over €200,00036.  
* **Reporting**: Public bodies must include GPP criteria in their tenders and submit annual reports to the EPA and SEAI40. Any omission of GPP criteria must be documented and explained in public databases40.  
* **Low-Carbon Specifications**: Under the EPBD, public bodies must specify low-carbon construction components and sustainable processes, drawing on Construction Products Regulation data and standard EN 15978:201140.  
* **Life-Cycle Costing**: Procurers are directed to evaluate bids based on the total cost of ownership—including operational energy use, maintenance, and end-of-life deconstruction or disposal costs—rather than just the lowest upfront purchase price22.

Circular 17/2025 includes a mandatory "SME Test" to ensure that environmental requirements do not create disproportionate administrative or financial barriers for smaller suppliers40. In addition, tenders are designed to promote social equity by encouraging engagement with social enterprises, supporting local apprentice training, and requiring compliance with domestic fair pay agreements16.

The convergence of industrial strategy, housing demands, and environmental regulations is reshaping the operational environment for Irish manufacturers through to 20301. For offsite construction manufacturers, this regulatory environment requires a shift from project-by-project fabrication to a standardized, data-driven manufacturing model9.

### **Immediate Pressure Points**

Offsite construction manufacturers must address several immediate regulatory requirements:

1. **The Green Public Procurement Mandate (Circular 17/2025)**: Public bodies are now required to include environmental criteria in tenders, meaning manufacturers must provide verified performance data to qualify for state-funded housing and infrastructure contracts40.  
2. **Whole Life-Cycle Greenhouse Gas Assessments**: Effective 1 June 2026, all publicly funded residential projects with budgets exceeding €30 million must complete comprehensive life-cycle carbon assessments, requiring detailed material and carbon data from manufacturers42.  
3. **NSAI Agrément Certification**: Public social housing projects require systems to be certified by the NSAI, creating a barrier to market entry for uncertified modular platforms9.  
4. **CBAM Compliance**: Importers of non-EU steel, cement, or aluminum must register with the EPA portal and provide verified installation-level carbon data to avoid penalty risks38.

### **Medium-Term Escalation to 2030**

Over the medium term, regulatory requirements will continue to expand:

* **January 2028**: Mandatory whole-life carbon assessments and embodied carbon reporting in BER certificates will apply to all new buildings larger than 1,000 square meters, expanding to cover all new buildings by January 203042.  
* **The CPR 2024 Working Plan**: Prefabricated elements and building kits will enter the CPR's harmonized technical specifications between 2027 and 2029, requiring manufacturers to integrate EPDs directly into their Declarations of Performance and Conformity32.  
* **Data Privacy and Monitoring**: As manufacturers adopt digital workflows, they must manage employee data in compliance with GDPR and prepare for the automated system rules of the EU AI Act by late 202719.

### **Strategic Positioning: The 2030 Compliant Manufacturer**

The most resilient offsite construction manufacturers operating in Ireland through to 2030 will likely share a specific profile:

* **Technical Validation**: Systems will be NSAI Agrément certified, with a verified 60-year durability rating and standardized, open-access structural data5.  
* **Environmental Data Readiness**: Products will be supported by verified, low-carbon EPDs compiled in accordance with EN 15804, allowing seamless integration into whole-life carbon assessments32.  
* **Digital Integration**: Factory workflows will be connected to Building Information Modelling (BIM) architectures, utilizing machine-readable Digital Product Passports and structured digital Declarations of Performance and Conformity32.  
* **Circular Resource Design**: Structural components will be designed for disassembly and reuse, utilizing standardized packaging and transport frames that comply with the PPWR to minimize commercial waste levies46.  
* **Supply Chain and Compliance Management**: Procurement teams will track carbon data across their supply chain, using localized or low-carbon raw materials to minimize CBAM tariff exposure8. At the same time, the business will maintain transparent ESG reporting to support the Scope 3 requirements of major clients and public procurers33.
