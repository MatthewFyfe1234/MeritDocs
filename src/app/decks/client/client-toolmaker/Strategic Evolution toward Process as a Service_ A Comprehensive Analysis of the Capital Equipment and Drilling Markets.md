# **Strategic Evolution toward Process as a Service: A Comprehensive Analysis of the Capital Equipment and Drilling Markets**

The global capital equipment landscape is currently navigating a fundamental transition from a traditional hardware-centric sales model to a sophisticated service-oriented framework. This evolution, often termed "servitization" or "Process as a Service" (PraaS), represents a paradigm shift where manufacturers no longer merely sell machinery but instead guarantee operational outcomes, uptime, and productivity through continuous data feedback loops and integrated contractor networks. This report provides an exhaustive examination of the market dynamics, technological adoption rates, and competitive structures within the drilling and heavy equipment sectors to support the strategic positioning of Merit, a platform designed to facilitate this industrial metamorphosis.

## **Leading Drilling Equipment Manufacturers and Digital Strategies**

The major original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) in the drilling sector are aggressively pivoting toward digital differentiation. The focus has shifted from mechanical specifications to the "intelligence" of the machine and its ability to integrate into broader operational ecosystems.

### **Sandvik: The Digital and Automation Shift**

Sandvik has positioned digitalization as a core strategic priority, aiming for SEK 6.5 billion in digital offering revenues by 2025\.11 In 2024, Sandvik reached its goal of having 60% of its customers utilizing automated and digital solutions a full year ahead of schedule.11 Their platform ecosystem includes:

* **AutoMine® and OptiMine®**: Platforms for autonomous operations and real-time monitoring of underground mining fleets. In 2024, Sandvik acquired Universal Field Robots to strengthen its OEM-agnostic approach, allowing its automation software to integrate with third-party equipment.11  
* **SAM by Sandvik**: A digital platform for crushing and screening equipment that saw a 40% increase in usage rate in 2024, with the number of active users nearly doubling.12  
* **Remote Monitoring as a Service**: A new business model launched in 2024 by Sandvik Rock Processing Solutions, providing customers with predictive insights and optimization recommendations.11

### **Epiroc: Innovation and Electrification**

Epiroc’s strategy emphasizes the "Smart" in SmartROC, focusing on automation and data transparency. Their MTVR mobile power pack, introduced in 2023, exemplifies a shift toward providing operational agility by enabling electric blast-hole drills to operate without high-voltage cable management.14 Epiroc also focuses on "Zero Entry" strategies, where remote monitoring platforms like ConSite Mine enable the removal of personnel from hazardous drilling environments.14

### **Boart Longyear: Moving the Lab to the Core Yard**

Through its Veracio division, Boart Longyear has pioneered the integration of geological data services directly into the drilling process. Their TruScan™ system provides near real-time geochemical data and high-definition imagery of drill core at the rig site.15

* **Real-Time Geochemistry**: TruScan utilizes X-ray Fluorescence (XRF) to provide assays on the same day the core is drilled, allowing geologists to terminate or extend holes based on data rather than waiting weeks for lab results.15  
* **Data Serialization**: The platform standardizes imagery and geochemical data, making it "data science ready" for machine learning and 3D modeling.16

### **Vermeer and Ditch Witch: Precision in HDD**

In the HDD sector, Vermeer and Ditch Witch have developed tools that focus on jobsite planning and execution accuracy.

* **VermeerOne™ and BorePlan**: These platforms integrate project data, maintenance plans, and horizontal drilling plans into a single interface. Vermeer's "Auto Drill" technology on drills like the D550 offers automated control over rotation and thrust, optimizing performance and tool life.18  
* **Orange Intel (Ditch Witch)**: A telematics solution that captures operating parameters such as mud flow, thrust, and force. This data allows contractors to provide more accurate bids by understanding how machines perform across different terrains.20

## **Existing Software Solutions and the "Fragmented" Landscape**

While OEMs have made significant progress in developing proprietary telematics, the drilling contractor level remains plagued by software fragmentation. Most contractors operate mixed-brand fleets, leading to a situation where they must manage multiple, non-integrated IT systems.

### **Limitations of Current Contractor Software**

The industry still struggles with "data silos," where information from rig-site reporting systems is not shared with back-office ERP or maintenance management systems.21

* **Poor Information Flow**: Fragmented solutions result in inaccurate, cumbersome data retrieval and reduced decision-making capability. Many cost-management tasks are still conducted manually in Excel.21  
* **Rig-Site Focus**: Many existing rig site information systems focus solely on the immediate drilling process, creating delays when rig personnel require remote expertise from the home office.22  
* **Communication Gaps**: Remote drilling sites often suffer from unstable satellite communications, making the maintenance of a single, synchronized database between offshore and onshore sites difficult.21

### **Specialized Field Management Platforms**

Several independent software providers have emerged to address these gaps by offering cloud-based, data-driven platforms:

* **CorePlan and Krux Analytics**: These platforms focus on digitizing "plods" (daily reports), managing contracts, and automating invoicing to improve cash flow.23  
* **DrillOps and DrillPlan (SLB)**: SLB offers a suite of integrated digital drilling solutions that use AI to execute autonomous operations and decode drilling data into actionable insights, reportedly achieving an 80% reduction in non-productive time (NPT) in specific offshore applications.24

## **Outcome-Based Models: Equipment as a Service (EaaS)**

Equipment as a Service (EaaS) is a long-term, fleet-based service model where customers do not buy a machine but rather purchase "operating hours" or "operational outcomes".30 This model effectively shifts the burden of maintenance, uptime, and technical risk from the end-user to the manufacturer or dealer.

### **Volvo CE: A Template for EaaS**

Volvo offers an EaaS model where customers purchase operating hours at a fixed hourly rate. This approach provides several distinct advantages:

* **Operational Flexibility**: Users scale usage up or down based on project demands without the capital risk of ownership.30  
* **Uptime Guarantees**: Volvo manages the fleet and guarantees its availability throughout the agreement. If forecasted fleet hours are exceeded, Volvo may even pay a credit back, further reducing the net cost per hour.30  
* **Cash Flow Efficiency**: Cost and revenue generation move closely together, converting a capital expenditure (CAPEX) into a predictable operating expense (OPEX).30

### **Economic and Strategic Drivers of EaaS**

The EaaS market is projected to grow at a 6% CAGR through 2032, driven by flexible financing and the maturation of IoT technology.33 McKinsey estimates the total potential for EaaS in manufacturing to be USD 2 trillion.34

* **Risk Sharing**: EaaS aligns the interests of OEMs and customers, as both profit from higher equipment performance and reliability.31  
* **Predictable Revenue**: For OEMs, EaaS provides a stable recurring revenue stream that is 25% higher on average over the product lifecycle compared to a one-time product sale.31

### **Insurance and Risk Governance: The Data Dividend**

The availability of granular, "tamper-proof" field data enables a secondary economic benefit in the form of reduced insurance premiums. By providing insurers with verified usage and safety data, operators can move from demographic-based pricing to individual, performance-based pricing models.

* **Verified Safety Reductions**: Data from the Association of British Insurers (ABI) indicates that premium savings of **20–40%** are achievable for operators who can provide consistently high safety scores and verified behavior data.  
* **Commercial Vehicle Benchmarks**: Documented safety programs, such as verified speed governance and real-time monitoring, are valued by commercial insurers as worth a **20–40% premium reduction** for heavy vehicle fleets.  
* **Accident Frequency Impact**: Fleets utilizing telematics and AI-enabled monitoring have reported accident rate reductions of up to 40%, directly translating to lower loss ratios and a **15–35%** range for premium discounts.  
* **Claims Handling Efficiency**: Equipment owners with GPS and usage logs can resolve insurance claims **40–60% faster** by providing immediate, objective evidence of loss circumstances.

## **Strategic Synthesis and Market Implications**

The research indicates a profound structural imbalance in how the drilling and capital equipment sectors are currently serviced. While OEMs provide highly advanced, machine-specific telematics, the contractor—who must coordinate these machines with labor and third-party contractor networks—is left with fragmented, siloed data.

### **The Opportunity for "Process as a Service"**

The transition to "Process as a Service" requires a software platform that can transcend machine-level telemetry to orchestrate the entire field process. Key drivers for this transition include:

* **Labor Scarcity**: Automation and remote monitoring are no longer optional but necessary to mitigate the 25% industry-wide labor shortage.25  
* **Outcome-Based Expectations**: Large customers are moving toward guaranteed results. For example, in mining, customers like Barrick are rolling out remote monitoring services to entire global fleets to ensure productivity and grade control.13  
* **Economic Resilience**: Recurring revenue models like EaaS provide a more stable financial base for OEMs, protecting them from the boom-and-bust cycles of traditional equipment sales.31

For Merit, the competitive advantage lies in the ability to unify these disparate layers—telematics, field management, and contractor coordination—into a single intelligence hub that supports the "outcome as a service" promise. The explosive growth of the HDD market (13.5% CAGR) and the intensive drilling requirements of the mining sector ($10.5B market size) provide immediate, high-value entry points for such a solution.

### **Financial and Operational Challenges to Overcome**

The implementation of "Process as a Service" is not without obstacles. Manufacturers must refine their financial structures to accommodate pre-financing assets and managing reverse logistics for equipment returns.34 Furthermore, there is significant resistance to change and technical debt within many field-based organizations, requiring software platforms to be highly intuitive and offer immediate, tangible productivity gains (e.g., the 35% productivity increase and 25% maintenance cost reduction reported by some fleet management users).49

In conclusion, the convergence of high-frequency telematics, cloud-based process orchestration, and the economic shift toward OPEX models has created a definitive market opening for platforms that can deliver on the "Process as a Service" vision. The data confirms that the most successful players in the coming decade will be those who can transform raw field data into guaranteed operational outcomes.
